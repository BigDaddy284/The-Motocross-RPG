Open the game's track data

Click Open folder… and pick the repo folder or its public folder (public/data works too, without the tile art). The editor uses:

  • data/tracks.json, the tracks and venues it edits,
  • data/trackParts.json for track kinds, surfaces, ground and scenery, and data/racing.json for the section types (both read only), and
  • assets/art for the ground and scenery tiles, the same art as the map editor.

In Chrome or Edge, Save writes straight back into the folder. In other browsers, Save downloads tracks.json for you to copy into place.

New, Duplicate and Import make mod tracks: the first Save asks for the track's name, and each one is saved as its own file in public/mods/tracks/ (never into the game's tracks.json), to race from Mod Track in the game.

Features

Jumps, whoops, sand and the like go on top of the route: pick one and click the route to place it. Drag it along the route, drag its white diamonds to stretch it, Del removes it. Corners are features too: nothing is typed from the route's shape, and a feature rides as its type whatever shape it is drawn. Between features the route rides as plain track.
Drag the points to shape the route. Double-click the route to add a point, right-click a point to remove it. Select a point and Round corner to give it curve handles; drag a handle to shape the curve (Alt moves one side only).

Paint the ground round the track. Left-click paints, right-click puts the base ground back, Shift+drag fills a rectangle. The edge sets join grass to mud, gravel, concrete or long grass: each names where the second ground is in the cell. The mixed tiles show one ground through another in patches, and each joins up with itself.
Pick an item: it follows the pointer, see-through, where a click would place it; Q E turn it first. Click an item to select it, drag to move it (arrow keys nudge it), Q E turn it 15°, with Shift 5°, with Ctrl 1° (or type any angle in Turn; left empty, a marshal, flag man, pit crew, fan or TV crew faces the track on its own), Del removes it. Shift-click adds items to the selection and Shift-drag on open ground boxes them (Ctrl+A all); a selection drags, nudges, turns, copies and deletes together. Ctrl+C copies, Ctrl+X cuts, Ctrl+V pastes at the pointer, Ctrl+D duplicates. Snap object joins it onto a nearby item, end to end or side by side; Alt drops the snapping. Snapped, Q E swing it round the join, so a fence can turn off another and stay joined. The white arrow shows which way it faces.
Raise and lower the track along its length. Hover the track: the stretch the tool would change is highlighted (the length you set, centred under the pointer, following the route round corners). A click raises it by the height set, with the most at the centre easing to nothing at both ends; right-click lowers it instead. Nothing changes until you click, and each click is one undo. The track keeps its width, banking and berms, the ground either side rises or sinks to meet it as a grass slope, and riders slow up climbs and run quicker down drops.

Track editor help

  • Layout: the route is the shape the race screen shows: control points on a 100 × 60 grid, joined in order and back to the first point, where the finish line goes. Drag a point to move it (snaps to whole grid units; untick Snap grid or hold Alt for tenths). Double-click the route to add a point, right-click a point or press Del to delete it (the route joins up across it), arrow keys nudge it. Round corner R gives the selected point curve handles (blue squares): drag one to shape the curve, its mirror follows, Alt drags one side on its own; Sharp corner takes them off. − Segment takes out the stretch after the selected point, so the route is open there. The drawing the game races is generated from the route; between the features it rides as plain track whatever its shape, and Undo and Redo cover every step, each point placed included.
  • New tracks start empty and as a draft, drawing their route: click the ground point by point, and click the first point (the green ring) to close the route; Enter or Esc stops drawing, Backspace takes the last point off, and Draw route carries on from the last point of an open route. Close the route joins the last point back to the first; Open the route takes the closing stretch out again. Nothing else is laid for you: the start straight and the finish line are placed on their own under Start and finish, before or after. Features (corners, hairpins, berms, ruts, jumps, whoops, sand and the like) go on top of the route, and only where you put them: nothing is typed from the route's shape, so a route with no features rides as plain track. Pick one and it follows the pointer along the route, , . make it shorter or longer, a click places it; drag it along the route, drag its white diamonds to stretch it, Del removes it, and its line and jump height are under Sections. A feature rides as its type whatever shape it is drawn (a hairpin at 90° or wide open), and moving or reshaping it never changes its type. Two that overlap are allowed with a warning in Checks that names them (click it to select the feature). A draft saves at any time, with one point, an open route, no start or finish, or errors; the Route box says Draft, Draft with warnings, Ready for validation or Race-ready, and the game leaves drafts out until Ready to race passes the game's checks. Tracks made before features keep their sections as saved; Use features (Sections), or placing a feature, keeps their corners, jumps and special sections as features, with their types, and lets the route between them ride as plain track.
  • Start straight: every track starts from a gate on its own start straight that joins the route, as on a real track. Its points are the blue squares: the first is the gate (the black bar, with the yellow line-up behind it), the white one is where it joins the route and stays on the route when dragged. To add one, pick Start straight under Start and finish (or + Start straight above the view): it follows the pointer, its end (the ring) snapping onto the route where the pointer is near it, green where it can go and red with the reason where it can't; Q E turn it, F brings it in from the other side, , . make it shorter or longer, a click or Enter places it and Esc cancels. On an empty track it can go first, anywhere, and be joined on later by dragging its white end onto the route (anywhere on it, just before or after the finish line included). It turns red when it is off the route. Move start straight places it again, and Remove start straight takes it off. Riders ride it once, then the lap from the join round to the finish line, which ends lap 1. The first corner is the start straight's end: the stretch between its last two blue points, drawn blue and labelled, which the race rides with the first corner's own rules (difficulty, skills, lap 1's first-corner incident). It is not a section: there is none to place or pick, and a First corner section on an older track rides as a flat corner. Select a blue point for + Point and − Point. The checks ask for it to be at least 12 grid units long, clear of the route until it joins, meeting the route at 75° or less.
  • Finish line: its own part of the track, placed by you and never for you: closing the lap or joining the start straight makes no finish line. A tabletop or double can be the finish line instead: tick Finish line in its panel and the lap starts and ends at its foot, with the finish arch built over it on the take-off lip of its front hump, its posts on the banks beside the track and its banner high enough that a rider still in the air goes under it; moving the finish line elsewhere makes it a plain jump again, and a scenery finish arch is not needed (ticking the box takes one away). Pick Finish line under Start and finish (or + Finish line above the view) once the lap is closed and a chequered bar follows the pointer, snapping across the lap; a click or Enter places it. It becomes the lap's first point (a point is added there if there was none), and the sections are split and turned round to start there, so the lap is laid out exactly as before. Finish here puts it on the selected point, dragging point 0 moves it along the lap, and Remove finish line takes it off (the track is then unfinished until one is placed again). A track without one is a valid draft; it is listed under the checks as still needed to race. The finish arch (Scenery) always spans the track's width, its posts just outside the lips, and belongs on the finish line: placing the line moves it there, square to the track, its panel has Put on the finish line, and the checks say when it is off.
  • Kind sets how the race screen shows the track: a stadium track fits on one screen; outdoor and endurance tracks use a camera that follows the riders, with a map of the whole lap. Width is the track width on the race screen.
  • No-Turn Takedown (a Kind) is a straight drag strip on a long, slim 200 × 30 grid: four riders at a time drop off a raised start platform and race one run to the finish line, in knockout heats after a timed seeding run, with a best-of-three final. There are no bends anywhere, so the route is never drawn by hand: pick Start platform under Start and finish and click where the start line goes (the foot of the drop-in), Q / E turn it 5°, F turns it round, , . shorten or lengthen the run. + Straight and − Straight lengthen or shorten the run by a straight piece (10 grid units), the finish line moving on straight ahead. Features (jumps, whoops, rhythm and the rest) go on the run only. A tabletop or double at the end of the run can be the finish line (Finish line under the feature): it moves to end on the line, with the finish arch over it. Past the finish line is the run-off (the first section, yellow), where riders pull up; the way back from it is never drawn. The platform has four gates, two each side of the starter's box, and no start board official.
  • Move whole track picks the whole track up as one piece, on any Kind: it follows the pointer, and a click drops it, with its start, features, scenery, ground paint and heights. It moves in whole ground cells, so the paint stays under it.
  • Enduro Sprint (a Kind) is a timed test ridden one rider at a time, a few seconds apart, the lowest total time over the laps winning; a one-lap timed qualifying sets the start order. It follows its own building rules instead of a start straight: close the route, place the Finish line, then pick Start line under Start and finish and put it on the route where riders leave from. The stretch from the finish line round to the start line becomes the return lane (the first section, yellow): a single-file lane a quarter of the track's width, with stakes and tape down both sides, ridden at half speed and never timed, where riders queue for their next lap. It needs at least 20 grid units for the queue and must never cross or run into the test; there is no start gate, no start straight and no start board official. The race view builds the start on the start line: a sponsor arch with a timing beam under it, the clock, the timekeeper with a stopwatch and the scorer with a clipboard; place a Sprint start from Scenery to choose its words and colour (it snaps onto the start line). The enduro sections go on any track as features: Rock garden, Tree roots, Log crossing (four logs across the track), Log step (one big log), Water crossing (a stream across the track and out into the grass, with spray), Off-camber (the track falls away to the outside of the corner), Hill climb (a long steep climb to a crest) and Woods singletrack (trees close on both sides). Boulders and Log piles are trackside scenery to dress them.
  • Sections are what the race simulation rides, in order. Each is placed on the drawing by its share of the lap length and ridden as its type whatever shape is drawn there; the panel says what the shape reads as (straight, sweeper, corner or hairpin). On a track from before features, From drawing replaces the sections with straights and corners that suit the drawing, if you ask for it. Click the track to pick the section under the pointer. Drag a white diamond to move metres between two sections. The section box sets the line riders take through it and the height of jumps and hills. Supercross jumps (from the jump chart): Triple, Quad, Step-up, Step-down, Step-on step-off (a single, a tabletop with a lip on its end, a single; Step-on with two or three tables puts more tabletops between, hopped lip to table), On-off, Single over table, Table over single, Wall jump, Ski jump, Rhythm section, Rollers and a Dragon's back (four humps each bigger than the last, building up to a big final mound launched off). Riders take each jump their own way by their jumping skill and the lap: a tabletop cleared or landed short and ridden off, a triple doubled then singled, a quad as a quad, a double-double or single-double-single, and a mistake comes up short on the face. Tunnels: a tabletop, step-up or step-on has a Tunnel choice: The track passes under it when another part of the route is drawn across its deck (the flat top in the middle), so riders there go through the tunnel while the rest jump over, or A pedestrian tunnel. Both sides of the deck become concrete walls with the opening and a portal frame, and the map draws the hole as a dark band. Choosing the track tunnel makes the feature at least 22 grid units long and 3.5 m high; the checks say when nothing passes under the deck, when the deck is too short for the lower track, or when the jump is too low.
  • Gates is the start bonus curve, in percent of start time, best gate first (Spec 10.4).
  • Surfaces: mud, sand, water and other patches along the lap, laid by clicking the track: the stretch a click would surface follows the pointer in the surface's colour. Click a patch to select it and drag it along the lap; its start and length can be typed in too.
  • Ground: paint the ground round the track with the game's ground tiles.
  • Scenery: banners (with your own words and colour), sponsor boards, start gates, tuff blocks, hay bales, tyre walls, stands, crowds, pits, trees and more. Paddock vehicles are the game's own vans at the race bikes' scale: an old rusty truck (empty, or one or two bikes on the back), an old rusty van (doors shut or open, a bike beside it), the MX vans and sports motorhomes (closed up, awning out, or bikes on stands under it), the factory hauler (closed, or a three-sided awning the length of the trailer with bikes under it) and a bike on a stand. Each has a colour and a set-up picked for it, like a fan's shirt; select it to choose them, and the vehicle's body alone counts as solid (the awning side is the lower side on the map before turning). Trackside items are built the same way, each with a colour, a design and a set-up picked for it unless chosen: the TV crew (camera operators standing or kneeling, a tripod camera, a camera van with a crew on its roof, a TV tower, a camera crane, a drone pilot, photographers standing, kneeling or lying in the dirt, a commentary box, a big screen, a press tent); the pits (a pit tent set up with bikes on stands, mechanics and riders, both, or the tool chest, generator and cans, with the team's name on its valance; a bike wash, a bike trailer with one or two bikes, pit clutter, a trade stand, a merchandise trailer, a catering van with a queue, a hospitality marquee, an enduro time check, a signing table); what stands over and beside the track (a pedestrian bridge over the track, empty or with fans crossing, its width set like a banner's and only its stair towers solid; a timing beam and a sponsor inflatable arch, over the track the same way; crowd barriers, cones, marshals (standing trackside, on a pallet stand, in a roofed tower, a pair with an extinguisher, or behind hay bales: each watches a stretch of track and keeps the yellow flag down until a rider goes down there, then leans out and waves it until the rider is away; Set watch then a click on the track sets how far along the lap they watch, at most 36 grid units, drawn as a yellow band, and with no end set they watch the next 18 units), the medics and their ambulance, a marshal quad, a track tractor pulling the harrow or towing the water bowser, a skid steer loader, portable toilets in ones, twos or fours, feather flags, a PA speaker pole, a VIP grandstand with a roof; and the supercross kit: a podium (motocross boxes and a board, or a lit supercross stage; empty, the top three, or champagne), a holeshot board, pyro flame pods, a lighting truss over the track, a stadium tunnel and a trophy table); the finish line crew: a flag man on any of the marshals' stands, placed just before the finish line, who holds up the white flag as the leader comes up to start the last lap, then puts it down and takes up the chequered flag as the leader comes round to finish, holding it until the last rider still racing has gone by; and pit crew (standing, behind a pit wall or with a mechanic), placed just after the line, each given a rider in a race and leaning out with the pit board (place and laps to go) as that rider comes by every lap; Checks mentions either standing more than 12 grid units from the line; and the fans (in camping chairs, taking pictures, round a lunch table under a parasol, on a picnic blanket, under a parasol, leaning on a fence). People face the track unless the item is turned; items with words take your own text. Pick an item and it follows the pointer, see-through, on the map and in the race view, where a click would place it, turned as Q and E have turned it (the turn is kept for the next item too). Q and E turn the selected item 15° a press, 5° with Shift and 1° with Alt, and the Turn box takes any whole number of degrees; Ctrl+D duplicates it, the copy standing end to end with it (so pressing again runs a fence on) while Snap object is ticked. The arrow keys nudge it half a grid unit (2 with Shift). Selecting an item makes its kind the one picked in the palette, so the next click places another like it. Shift-click adds items to the selection, Shift-drag on open ground boxes every item inside, and Ctrl+A selects them all; a selection drags, nudges, turns, duplicates and deletes together, and Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V copy, cut and paste it, pasting with its middle at the pointer (on the map or in the race view). With Snap object ticked, an item placed or dragged near another joins it end to end or side by side with their edges flush, and turned the same way if it is already within 12° of lining up (turned further by hand, it keeps its own angle and only its position snaps); the item it joins gets a dashed outline, so a run of fences, walls or stands lines up; hold Alt to drop it for one move, as for the grid. A white arrow on the item being placed or selected, on the map and in the race view, shows which way it faces: people, stands, boards and tents face the arrow, vehicles point their nose along it, and a marshal, flag man, pit crew, fans or TV crew not turned by hand face the track (the arrow shows where), so you can see before you click. While it is snapped, and once it is placed with an end joined onto another item, Q and E swing it round the join instead of its middle, so a fence or wall can turn off in another direction and stay joined (with both ends joined it keeps the end on the item placed first). Cheering fans are people who stand trackside and react as bikes pass: pick a variant (body shape), an action (arms up, wave, clap, fist pump, celebrate) and a shirt colour, or leave them to be picked. Draw route gives a fan a walk in: when the track loads they walk from where placed along it, at walking pace, and cheer from its end, so a crowd can file in. A fan must never be on the track, where placed or where the walk ends (an error the game refuses); a walk that crosses the track is only a warning. Each fan reacts in their own time, with a rest between, so a crowd never moves as one.
  • Start board official: pick Start board official under Start and finish in Layout, or the last item in Scenery, and click where they wait with the board while the riders line up (one a track; without one the game picks their spot beside the start straight). Select them and click Draw route, then click the ground in order to where they end up (the red point), and Done or Esc; Cancel puts the old route back, Reset route clears it. Drag a route point to move it, right-click it or press Del to remove it; dragging the official moves only where they start. In the countdown they raise the board and set off at five, turn it sideways at three and lower it white side out at one as they arrive. The route can be at most official.maxWalk grid units (trackParts.json, 8), may cross the track ahead of the gate but not the riders lined up at the gate or solid scenery, and must end clear of the track; the race view runs the game's checks and they show under the official and in Checks. On a new track they stand where placed until there's a start straight; Checks says when a track has no official or an official has no route.
  • Height: Raise the track and Lower the track work along the track's length: hover the track and the stretch the tool would change is highlighted, the Length you set centred under the pointer and following the route round corners (cut at the end of an open route); a click raises it by the Height set, right-click lowers it, with the most at the centre easing to nothing at both ends, so the hill meets the track either side with no step and no kink. Nothing changes until you click; each click is one undo, and clicking the same stretch again steps its height instead of stacking another hill (back to level, it goes). Hills are saved with the track (hills), keep their place when the route is reshaped, and are drawn on the map as a band with their height. The track keeps its width, banking, berms and section types; the ground either side rises or sinks to meet it as a grass slope, never a wall. The ground brush (raise, lower, smooth, flatten; radius and strength) shapes the ground away from the track as before. Heights go from 36 m down to 36 m up; riders slow up climbs and run quicker down drops; ground steeper than 45° is refused as a wall, and a track climbing or dropping more than 35% along its length is a warning. Old tracks keep their ground as saved.
  • Race view: you edit in the game's own race camera and 3D scene with hills, jumps, scenery and ground as a race draws them, and the lap map in its corner. Click, drag and right-click on the 3D ground; the keys are the same in every mode. Mouse wheel or + − zoom, W A S D or a middle-button (or Space+drag) pan, [ ], the ⟲ Turn left and ⟳ Turn right buttons (bottom left) or a Shift+middle-button drag turn the view round the track, to see it from any side; 0 or Reset view goes back to the race camera's angle. Turning only changes the editor's view: clicks still land where the pointer is, and races always use the fixed race camera. The selection is orange, problems red or dashed yellow. In Layout each section shows as a stripe in its kind's colour, with a cream line across the track where one section ends and the next begins. No race runs while you edit. Opened straight from disk, the race view runs the game from the published site (bigdaddy284.github.io/Motocross-RPG), so it needs an internet connection; with neither that nor npm run dev, the editor shows a plain map instead.
  • Modes: 1 Layout, 2 Surfaces, 3 Ground, 4 Scenery, 5 Height.
