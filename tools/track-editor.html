Click Open folder… and pick the repo folder or its public folder (public/data works too, without the tile art). The editor uses:

  • data/tracks.json, the tracks and venues it edits,
  • data/trackParts.json for track kinds, surfaces, ground and scenery, and data/racing.json for the section types (both read only), and
  • assets/art for the ground and scenery tiles, the same art as the map editor.

In Chrome or Edge, Save writes straight back into the folder. In other browsers, Save downloads tracks.json for you to copy into place.

Track editor help

  • Layout: the drawing is the shape the race screen shows: points on a 100 × 60 grid, joined in order and back to the first point, which is the finish line. Drag a point to move it (snaps to whole grid units; untick Snap or hold Alt for tenths). Double-click the track to add a point, right-click a point or press Del to delete it, arrow keys nudge it. Round corner R swaps a sharp point for a curve.
  • Start straight: every track starts from a gate on its own start straight that joins the lap, as on a real track. Its points are the blue squares: the first is the gate (the black bar, with the yellow line-up behind it), the white one is where it joins the lap and stays on the lap when dragged. + Start straight adds one beside the first part of the lap. Riders ride it once, then the lap from the join to the finish line, which ends lap 1. Select a blue point for + Point and − Point. The checks ask for it to be at least 12 grid units long, clear of the lap until it joins, joining in the first half of the lap at 75° or less.
  • Kind sets how the race screen shows the track: a stadium track fits on one screen; outdoor and endurance tracks use a camera that follows the riders, with a map of the whole lap. Width is the track width on the race screen.
  • Sections are what the race simulation rides, in order. Each is placed on the drawing by its share of the lap length, and its type has to suit the shape drawn there (straight, sweeper, corner or hairpin, as racing.json says): a hairpin on a straight would have riders braking for nothing. A section that doesn't suit its drawing is edged in red and listed in the checks. From drawing replaces the sections with straights and corners that suit the drawing. Click the track to pick the section under the pointer. Drag a white diamond to move metres between two sections. The section box sets the line riders take through it and the height of jumps and hills.
  • Gates is the start bonus curve, in percent of start time, best gate first (Spec 10.4).
  • Surfaces: mud, sand, water and other patches along the lap, laid by clicking the track.
  • Ground: paint the ground round the track with the game's ground tiles.
  • Scenery: banners (with your own words and colour), sponsor boards, start gates, tuff blocks, hay bales, tyre walls, stands, crowds, pits, trees and more. Q and E turn the selected item, Ctrl+D duplicates it.
  • Modes: 1 Layout, 2 Surfaces, 3 Ground, 4 Scenery.
  • Export / Import save one track as a .track.json file and add one back.
  • Preview P rides a dot round the lap, slower through the harder sections.
  • Race it in the game opens a race day on the track with the full field, with npm run dev running. Save first.
  • Save Ctrl+S. Undo Ctrl+Z, Redo Ctrl+Y. An unchanged file saves back byte for byte. After saving, run npm test; the game also checks the file when it starts.
  • Development and Final v1: trackParts.json "sectionEditor" shows the section editing. Final v1 turns it off, leaving the track and surroundings editor; tracks keep their sections and race the same. Add ?final=1 to the address to try the Final v1 editor.
